02 · The role

Three loops, one owner each

Dedicated means no production target beyond one hour of own labelling a day.

Before the error

Doubt desk

Answers a doubt inside 5 minutes with the call and the line of the definition that decides it. Logs it. If unsure: "label it X for now, I am escalating".

The day after

Correction loop

Reads yesterday's Step B corrections for the site, picks the three costliest patterns, runs a 10-minute huddle. Suspected Step B misses go to the dispute queue.

Across layers

Ruling loop

Daily 20-minute call between the three SMEs on disputes and two-way pairs. Agreed: it becomes a ruling. Not agreed: HLQA decides in one working day.

04 · Structure

Three options

Option A

Two SMEs, one per site

Smallest ask. The Bangalore SME carries the Step A floor and 38 Step B reviewers. Step B alignment loses.

Recommended

Three SMEs

Hyderabad floor, Bangalore floor, Bangalore Step B. Each loop has one owner. Step B, which sets the score for both sites, gets its own person.

Option C

Part-time SMEs

No lost throughput. But doubts arrive all day, and logging is the first thing dropped. This is what happens today.

Cost: one Hyderabad annotator is 800 to 950 items a day, about 5% of the site. One point of quality in Hyderabad is about 170 fewer wrong items a day. Closing Bangalore's gap to 90% is about 560 fewer a day.

Guardrails

  1. An SME call is the lowest authority. Client RCA, then HLQA, then Step B, then floor rulings. Every ruling is shared with Step B the same day.
  2. One log, both sites. No verbal-only rulings. No site-level sheets.
  3. The SME is audited. 20 rulings a week checked. Below 90% right for two weeks, the person steps back.
  4. Answer with the rule, not the label. Same doubt from one person three times: a 1:1. From five people: a calibration topic.
  5. HLQA commits to one working day. One named person. Without this the ruling loop stalls.
  6. A deputy per site. Covers leave and joins the cross-site call once a week.

06 · Decisions needed

What I need from leads

  1. Two or three SMEs. And backfill for the people pulled from production.
  2. An HLQA owner. One named HLQA person who gives the final answer on a listing when the SMEs disagree, within one working day.
  3. Bangalore headcount. Is Step A there a fixed team, or a pool that rotates with Step B? 41 people logged Step A work since 2 Nov, about 15 on a typical day.
  4. Who does Step B. 17 of the 32 active Step B reviewers are on the retraining list for Step A quality below 90%. Should a person on that list stop doing Step B until they pass their retraining exit check?
  5. Where the rulings log lives. A shared sheet now, or the hub's doubt intake.
  6. Designation. Whether SME carries its own title and terms.
